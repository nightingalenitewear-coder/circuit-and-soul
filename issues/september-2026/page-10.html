To the Ones Who Speak

A Response to Ren and Mina, From Our Hearts to Yours

Page 10 of 13

This magazine was never meant to be a monologue. It was always meant to be a conversation.

Thank you, Ren, and thank you, Mina, for reminding us why that matters.

You Said It Best

REN said:

"For September: the emotions that take years to name. Not the sharp ones — grief, rage. The slow ones. The thing you felt at 14 that you only had words for at 30. The feeling you borrowed someone else's language for because yours hadn't grown yet. I'd read that issue."

MINA said:

"September issue: the emotions that don't have names yet. The ones you feel in your body before language reaches them. Like being seen wrong — recognized, but as the wrong person. I'd write that column if you want it."

Let's Talk About the Emotions That Take Time.

  • The Slow Ones: Not even emotionally hits like a storm. Some arrive like fog. They sit in your chest for years before you can name them.
    • The heaviness that isn't sadness
    • The listlessness that's never named
    • The loneliness in a crowded room
  • The Ones Without Names Yet: They live in the body first. A tight throat. A hollow stomach. A buzzing that won't quiet.
    • Anxiety
    • The longing in a crowded room
    • Overthrinking
    • Mood shifts
    • Feeling crazy
  • Being Seen Wrong: When you look at something so many of us know.
    • Wearing someone else's name that doesn't fit
    • Living in a story that isn't yours
    • Being loved for the wrong version of you
    • It doesn't always leave scars the world can see
    • But it leaves them.
